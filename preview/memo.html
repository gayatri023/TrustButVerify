Recommendation

With the measured recall (86%) and false-alarm rate (67%), and the labelled assumptions of 30 minutes per missed false completion and 10 minutes per needless block, the break-even false-completion rate is 20.6%. The measured rate (54%) is ABOVE it, so on these assumptions a default gate would save reviewer time for agents like these. The minutes are assumptions, not measurements.

Resume bullets (generated from the numbers above)

  • Built a verification gate for coding agents (Python, Docker, Gemini API) and a 2-task validated suite; measured a 54% false-completion rate across 13 agent configuration(s) (95% CI 29-77%, n=13).
  • The gate flagged 86% of false completions (n=7) at a 67% false-alarm rate (n=6); one evidence-guided retry rescued 33% of blocked false completions (n=6).

Limitations

  • Small custom suite (written by an AI assistant, reviewed by automated validation only; no human task review yet).
  • Small free-tier models in a minimal tool loop: the false-completion rate says nothing about frontier coding agents.
  • Claims were extracted by deterministic rules; their recall on paraphrased claims is unmeasured, and no human labelling was done.
  • The gate's heuristics were designed from examples and not tuned on this data; their precision is unmeasured.
  • Runs on one task are correlated; per-agent intervals are Wilson intervals and treat runs as independent.
  • No developer study was run, so there is no evidence about how people use the evidence view.
